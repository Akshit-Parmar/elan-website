01 / THE ASSIGNMENT

You have the brief.
Not all the answers.

Your team represents a fictional country facing a crisis. There are resources to manage, other nations to negotiate with and people depending on your decisions.

Then new information arrives. The plan you agreed on five minutes ago might no longer work.

Take your seat ↓
02 / THE CABINET

Three seats.
One shared decision.

One country, three responsibilities. Each person brings a different perspective. Your job is to turn those perspectives into a shared response.

SEAT 01 / DIRECTION

Head of State

Sets the direction, holds the team together, makes the final calls, and presents the national response with authority.

  • Sets the strategy and priorities for the crisis
  • Chairs every discussion and breaks deadlocks
  • Balances the priorities of both ministers
  • Leads the final presentation to the judges
SEAT 02 / RESOURCES

Finance Minister

Controls the national budget. Nothing the cabinet proposes happens unless it can be funded.

  • Manages the budget and resources across both days
  • Costs every proposal and flags what cannot be afforded
  • Allocates between relief, security and infrastructure
  • Defends the plan's financial feasibility
SEAT 03 / DIPLOMACY

Foreign Affairs Minister

Represents your country in the room. Builds alliances, negotiates support and works to ease tensions.

  • Manages relations with the other nations in the room
  • Leads negotiations for aid, alliances and agreements
  • Eases tensions when the crisis crosses borders
  • Keeps the cabinet's decisions from isolating the nation

PHASE 01 / INITIAL BRIEFING

Read the room.

Your country profile, resources and first intelligence packet are on the table. Work out what matters, divide responsibilities and agree on your first response.

  • Understand the crisis and your country's position
  • Set priorities across the three cabinet roles
  • Submit the Initial Response Brief
YOUR OUTPUT A first plan of action
PHASE 02 / ESCALATION

The facts have changed.

Fresh intelligence changes the picture. Other countries react. Public trust shifts. Decide what to keep, what to change and how to explain it.

  • Reassess the risks and available resources
  • Negotiate with other nations
  • Prepare your public response
YOUR OUTPUT A response that can adapt
PHASE 03 / FINAL RESPONSE

Stand behind your choices.

Bring the cabinet's work together. The top teams present their response to the panel, answer questions and explain the decisions that brought them here.

  • Submit the final plan
  • Present the cabinet's response
  • Defend it under questioning
YOUR OUTPUT A plan you can defend
04 / THE PANEL

Convince us it can work.

Judges look at the decisions behind your presentation: whether your plan can work, who it affects and how well your cabinet responds together.

Leadership

Calm direction, team coordination, initiative, and decision-making under pressure.

Strategy

Clear priorities, practical planning, smart trade-offs, and long-term thinking.

Realism

Feasible actions that consider resources, timelines, stakeholders, and consequences.

Ethics

Fair, responsible decisions that balance urgency with human impact.

Communication

Strong public messaging, clear presentation, and confident defense of choices.

Adaptability

The ability to respond when the situation changes and old plans stop working.
