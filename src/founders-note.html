What Élan
should deliver.

A note from Akshit Parmar, Founder of Élan Summit.

First edition5–6 September 2026
Mulshi, Pune

Students give an event their time, preparation and trust. Élan has to make that commitment worthwhile.

For me, that starts with the competition. A good brief should require students to examine the information, disagree with each other constructively and make a decision they can explain. In Crisis Command, a confident presentation has to be supported by a workable plan: what the country needs, what it can afford and what the cabinet is prepared to prioritise.

The event around that competition matters just as much. Participants need clear instructions, useful preparation material and a team they can approach when something is unclear. Schools and families need accurate information about arrangements before making a commitment. These are the standards I want Élan to be held to.

The first edition gave us experience to build on and practical lessons to act on. The responsibility now is to review what worked, address what did not and prepare the next edition carefully. Adding more activities means little if the essential arrangements are not sound.

As founder, my responsibility is to keep those priorities in view. The organising committee’s work should make it easier for participants to prepare, compete and enjoy the summit. Every department contributes to that experience.

For the next edition, our focus is Crisis Command. I want participants to leave able to explain a decision they made, a view they reconsidered and what their team achieved together. That is the experience we should work to deliver.

Akshit ParmarFounder, Élan Summit
