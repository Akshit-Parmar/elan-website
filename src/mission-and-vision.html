Our mission

Practise leadership through competition.

Our mission is to give students practice in making and defending decisions together. In Crisis Command, teams work with a brief, a set of resources and competing priorities. They have to agree on a response, explain the trade-offs and adapt when new information arrives.

Research, financial planning, negotiation and public speaking each contribute to that work. The aim is for every cabinet member to have a responsibility and understand how their work affects the team’s response.

Organising offers a second route to participation. Committee members work on defined tasks across finance, media, outreach, design and event operations. They have to meet deadlines, communicate changes and account for the work they submit.

02 / In practice

Two sides of the same summit.

Inside a cabinet

The Finance Minister may be able to fund relief or infrastructure, but not both. The Foreign Affairs Minister has to consider what neighbouring countries will do. The Head of State has to bring those views together. A good proposal is one the whole cabinet can defend.

Behind the scenes

A programme change reaches far beyond the person who makes it. Logistics needs a room ready, outreach needs the right information for schools, and the social media team needs to know what it can announce. Committee work gives students practice in knowing who else needs to hear from them.

Our vision

Make the summit worth returning to.

Our vision is to develop Élan into a recurring student summit with a clear format and dependable organisation. Each edition should give returning participants a new challenge while helping first-time participants understand where to begin.

That requires clear rules, preparation material that explains the roles, and judging criteria participants can understand. It also requires room for different strengths: careful research and sound financial reasoning should matter alongside confidence in speaking.

We want students from different schools to work as peers, question each other’s proposals and meet beyond the competition sessions. Future editions should develop that experience through participant feedback and a review of how the event was organised.
