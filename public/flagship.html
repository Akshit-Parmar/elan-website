The main attraction

Lead a nation under pressure.

Crisis Command is the signature experience of Élan. Mixed-school teams act as the emergency governing council of a fictional country facing a national crisis. The scenario evolves across two days, forcing students to think fast, negotiate, plan, communicate, and defend decisions under pressure.

It is not a debate, not a prepared speech, and not a normal quiz. It is a live strategy experience where every team must adapt when new information arrives.

The promise

Not an MUN. Not a debate. Not a conference.

Crisis Command is an immersive crisis festival format where leadership, ethics, realism, communication, and teamwork are tested in real time.

Team roles

Three students. One council.

Each team works like an emergency cabinet. Every role has a different responsibility, so the strongest teams are not just loud, they are balanced, strategic, and coordinated.

01

Head of State

Sets the direction, holds the team together, makes final calls, and presents the national response with authority.

02

Finance Minister

Manages resources, budgets, relief priorities, trade-offs, and the economic consequences of every decision.

03

Foreign Affairs Minister

Handles diplomacy, global pressure, alliances, external communication, and international consequences.

Simulation flow

How the crisis unfolds.

The format is designed to build pressure gradually. Teams start with incomplete information, receive updates, face complications, and end by defending a final response.

Phase 01

Initial Briefing

Teams receive the country profile, crisis trigger, role responsibilities, available resources, and first intelligence packet.

  • Understand the crisis
  • Assign role priorities
  • Set first response goals
  • Submit the Initial Response Brief
Phase 02

Escalation Phase

New updates create pressure. The crisis expands, stakeholders react, public trust shifts, and teams must adapt quickly.

  • Revise strategy
  • Balance risk and feasibility
  • Prepare public communication
Phase 03

Final Response

Top teams present their complete response, defend decisions, answer questions, and show how they managed the crisis.

  • Submit final plan
  • Present to the panel
  • Defend under questioning
Judging

What wins Crisis Command.

The best team is not the one with the most dramatic speech. The winning response must be realistic, ethical, coordinated, strategic, and clearly communicated.

Leadership

Calm direction, team coordination, initiative, and decision-making under pressure.

Strategy

Clear priorities, practical planning, smart trade-offs, and long-term thinking.

Realism

Feasible actions that consider resources, timelines, stakeholders, and consequences.

Ethics

Fair, responsible decisions that balance urgency with human impact.

Communication

Strong public messaging, clear presentation, and confident defense of choices.

Adaptability

The ability to respond when the situation changes and old plans stop working.

Why students love it

It feels real.

Students do not just sit and listen. They enter a role, face uncertainty, work with people from other schools, and get tested by a changing situation.

Why schools value it

It builds visible skills.

Crisis Command develops communication, confidence, critical thinking, leadership, teamwork, policy reasoning, and pressure handling in one format.

Where it appears

Both days of Élan.

The simulation begins on Day 1 with the briefing, escalation, and Initial Response Brief submission. It then continues on Day 2 with deeper consequences, final planning, final submissions, presentations, and awards.
